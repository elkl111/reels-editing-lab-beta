# Hook patterns

Seven ways to open a loop. They're patterns, not templates: fill them with the reel's own payoff, tension and details. Each example shows a **lame** version (generic, hypey, or untrue) next to a **human** one (specific, curious, sounds like a person).

The niches below are made up to show range. Never reuse an example line as is.

---

## 1. Name the mistake
Point at something the viewer is probably doing wrong. It works because people can't resist checking whether it's them. Be precise about the mistake, and don't scold.

- Lame: "You're doing Instagram WRONG"
- Human: "Posting at 9am is why nobody sees it"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Bakery owner | "If your sourdough comes out flat, it's probably not your starter." | eyebrow FLAT SOURDOUGH? / text "It's the *fridge*, not the starter" |
| Freelance designer | "Most designers lose the client in the first email, not the pitch." | eyebrow FIRST EMAIL / text "Where you *actually* lose clients" |

## 2. Flip the belief
State what everyone assumes, then signal it's wrong without giving the answer. It works because it creates friction with something they already believe. The flip must be real and defended in the reel, not contrarian for show.

- Lame: "Everything you know about marketing is a LIE"
- Human: "More content isn't why you're not growing"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Fitness coach | "You don't need more willpower to work out in the morning." | eyebrow MORNING WORKOUTS / text "It was never *willpower*" |
| Solopreneur with AI | "The best AI tool isn't the one you think it is." | eyebrow STOP SWITCHING TOOLS / text "Build your *business brain*" |

## 3. Show the result, hide the how
Lead with the outcome and keep the method for the reel. It works because the gap between "it happened" and "how?" is the loop. The result must be real and stated plainly, with no inflated numbers.

- Lame: "How I made $10K in a week (insane method)"
- Human: "I answered 40 DMs in 10 minutes this morning"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Therapist | "My clients stopped cancelling last minute after one small change." | eyebrow NO MORE NO-SHOWS / text "One line in my *booking email*" |
| Photographer | "These were shot on my phone in a parking lot." | eyebrow PHONE ONLY / text "The *parking lot* trick" |

## 4. Call out who it's for
Name the viewer so precisely that they feel seen. It works through recognition: "that's me". It also filters out the wrong viewers, which helps reach. Use their situation, not a demographic.

- Lame: "Attention entrepreneurs!"
- Human: "If you have 3 AI tabs open right now…"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Copywriter | "If you rewrite your About page every three months, this is for you." | eyebrow ABOUT PAGE / text "Why it *never* feels right" |
| Coach | "For anyone who has a waitlist but hates selling." | eyebrow WAITLIST, NO SALES? / text "Say *this* instead" |

## 5. Start mid-thought
Open as if the conversation already started: a confession, a reaction, an aside. It works because it feels unscripted and human, the opposite of an ad. Cut any warm-up before it in the edit.

- Lame: "Hey guys, today I want to share something personal"
- Human: "Okay, I have to admit something about my morning routine"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Ceramicist | "I almost threw this whole batch away." | eyebrow KILN DISASTER / text "Then I *looked closer*" |
| Business owner | "I genuinely did not expect this to work." | eyebrow TRIED IT FOR A WEEK / text "The *boring* system that worked" |

## 6. Lead with the number
A specific number makes a claim concrete and promises structure. It works because it's precise, it's believable, and it sets a finish line. Use odd, real numbers ("3 things", "11 minutes"). Never round up for effect.

- Lame: "The TOP 10 secrets to success"
- Human: "3 prompts I use every single Monday"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Nutritionist | "Here's everything I eat before 11am, and it's 4 things." | eyebrow BEFORE 11AM / text "Just *4 things*" |
| Podcaster | "It takes me 12 minutes to turn one episode into a week of posts." | eyebrow ONE EPISODE / text "*12 minutes*, a week of posts" |

## 7. Before/after
Two states side by side, with the bridge left out. It works because a contrast is instantly readable and the change itself is the loop. Both states must be in the reel.

- Lame: "My life before and after this INSANE hack"
- Human: "I used to spend Sundays writing captions"

| Niche | Spoken first line | Card |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual assistant | "My inbox used to be 400 emails. Now it's under 10 every evening." | eyebrow 400 → 10 / text "What *changed*" |
| Yoga teacher | "I used to plan every class from scratch." | eyebrow CLASS PLANNING / text "Now it takes *one note*" |

---

## Quick self-check before presenting
- Could this hook go on someone else's reel? Then it isn't specific. Add a detail from this reel.
- Does the reel fully deliver it? If not, scale the promise down to what's there.
- Read it out loud. Would you text it to a friend? If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it in the speaker's own words.
- Is the answer guessable from the hook alone? Then hold something back.
- Are the three options three different patterns? If not, swap one.
